How much can we safely decide locally?

As people, AI systems, and machines share electricity, charging stations, and other limited resources, they face a practical question: what can each decide for itself, and when must everyone coordinate?

One approach is to agree on limits in advance, then let participants act within them. This is already used in several fields. The difficult part is knowing whether those limits remain useful and safe when demand changes, communication fails, or physical capacity disappears.

Society of Explorers is investigating whether there is a specific, worthwhile improvement to make—or whether existing methods already answer the question. We have not established a new protocol or a need for a new token.

Help us find the missing evidence, challenge an assumption, or show that a simpler answer is enough.

Opening note

What survived our first audit?

Our current question concerns local decisions under shared physical limits. The core idea has substantial prior art, including distributed resource accounting and electricity operating envelopes. We have not established a new protocol.

An earlier claim that individually feasible actions would remain safe together was rejected. A separate GPU-commitment experiment failed its stated overhead threshold. Neither result will be hidden to make the project look further along.

The next step is to find a real unmet need, specify the guarantees precisely, and compare with the strongest existing method. We welcome contributions that improve that investigation or show it should stop.

The GPU result was recovered from an existing lab record, not replicated in this audit. The accompanying arithmetic counterexamples are illustrations, not field trials. The evidence page carries the scoped negative-result summary. Raw lab files are not published here.

Three questions

Q1 · Where does waiting actually hurt? SOE-Q001 · Can you identify a real situation in which a device or operator must decide locally before a deadline, and an existing local controller or operating-envelope arrangement does not meet that need? Q2 · What happens when an agreed limit stops being safe? SOE-Q002 · For one concrete resource, what should a participant be allowed to do if its permission is still valid on paper but capacity has changed—or a revocation cannot reach it? Q3 · What really needs a shared record, and who remains responsible? SOE-Q003 · In a concrete arrangement between a resource operator, a participant, and a customer, what must be recorded or verified—and can ordinary controllers, databases, signed records, and contracts do the job?
