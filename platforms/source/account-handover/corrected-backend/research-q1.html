SOE-Q001 · version 1.0 · 27 September 2026

Where does waiting actually hurt?

Research status: Open question · Novelty unestablished · No field trial completed

The question

Can you identify a real situation in which a device or operator must decide locally before a deadline, and an existing local controller or operating-envelope arrangement does not meet that need?

Why it matters

A faster permission check is useful only if waiting causes a meaningful loss. Before proposing another mechanism, we need to understand a real task, its deadline, and what already works.

What is currently known

Existing systems can assign limits and let equipment operate within them. Some implementations also specify behavior during a communications outage. Local autonomy is therefore part of the comparison, not something we can simply claim to have invented.

What remains unknown

We have not identified a documented case where the proposed approach improves service enough to justify its added cost and complexity against the strongest existing alternative.

Contributions that would help

  • An example from operating a fleet, charging site, energy system, communications network, or another constrained service.
  • The decision that must be made, the deadline, what happens if it is missed, and the current fallback.
  • A source or anonymized trace showing a real delay or outage cost.
  • An existing product, paper, or operating practice that already solves the problem.

A useful first contribution: “At this kind of site, this decision has to happen within this time. Today we handle it this way. Here is where that works—or fails.” An observation without a paper is welcome; we will distinguish experience from general evidence.

What would change our minds

Evidence that existing arrangements meet the same requirement closes that case. A documented shortfall with a fair comparison and measurable consequences would justify a focused study.

Start with these sources

  • CSIP-AUS v1.2 explainer, May 2025, communication-loss behavior — dated implementation guidance. Reading status: the loss-of-communications section was read in the 27 September 2026 audit. Not independently reproduced. Not a claim about every current product.
  • AEMO Project EDGE operating-envelope research — practical allocation questions. Reading status: official report summaries inspected. Secondary report. Not independently reproduced.
  • SecuLEx — a preprint proposing exchange of operating limits. Reading status: relevant PDF text read, including market-timing assumptions. Not independently reproduced. A preprint is not a deployed product.

Claims
Fact within the cited sources: existing systems can assign limits and let equipment operate within them, and some specify communications-outage behavior. Unknown whether any documented case justifies another mechanism. Hypothesis that a specific improvement exists — not established. Not a fact that Society of Explorers has invented local autonomy.
Assumptions
A faster permission check matters only if waiting causes a meaningful loss. The comparison includes communication-loss defaults. It does not assume that disconnection leaves every conventional device waiting for a server.
Baseline
The strongest existing local controller or operating-envelope arrangement for the same task, including scheduled envelopes and a documented disconnection fallback. SecuLEx is a preprint proposal in that neighborhood, not a result that this question has beaten.

None. Contributions are not open yet. This is not a count of a community.
