Exception queue

Automation does not erase the desk; it changes what arrives there. When the ordinary loop runs itself, people often meet the exceptions — a limit excursion, a stale feed, or a schedule that no longer reconciles.

  • A limit excursion still needs a human disposition, not only an alarm tone.
  • A telemetry gap can mean pausing automation until the feed is trustworthy again.
  • A reconciled schedule mismatch still gets logged so the pattern stays visible.
  • Acknowledge, hold, and log are desk moves — exhibition texture for where humans still sit, not a copied control-room screen.

Wrong if a clean three-card schematic is taken as a universal interface or proof that a queue makes automation safe. Escalation, staffing, and authority vary. Human veto: a named person or accountable institution must still be able to pause, override, or reject the automated disposition.

Keep visible

  • Automation without a governor — cascade when price, protection, and liability disagree.
  • Smart contracts that settle without enforceability in the physical world.
  • Agents optimizing the wrong objective inside a live market.
  • Treating “electronic” as “government disappears” — it does not.
