When a Robot Can Pay Its Own Electric Bill

historical current extrapolation speculation

Thought experiment · companion note

A machine finishes work, settles for kilowatt-hours, and tries to buy compute — pressure-test, not prediction.

Companion to local flagship media · rights: chris-original · not published

A machine finishes work, checks its energy balance, and settles for the kilowatt-hours it burned — without a human typing a card number. That loop may stay fiction for a long time. The pieces already exist in fragments. This outline pressure-tests the loop; it does not predict the future.

1. Hook — settlement meets watts

Work requires energy. Energy on a real system requires delivery, metering, and settlement. Settlement requires a counterparty the market or utility recognizes. current The paradox is not “robots are people.” It is whether an autonomous agent can use some economic primitives — and what fails when it tries. speculation

2. Narrow claim (not equality)

Central question (machine-economy): What happens when intelligent machines can participate directly in economic systems?

Narrow claim only: could a non-human agent use some primitives we already extend to humans and corporations — identity, contracts, wallets, reputation, energy and compute access, physical agency — without claiming moral or legal personhood? speculation Curiosity over prophecy.

3. Unit R — thought experiment, not a product

Call the agent Unit R. Industrial-schematic, not cute. It has a task queue (inspect, haul, compute — keep generic). To finish, it needs energy. To operate tomorrow, it needs a way to settle. Somewhere in the chain, a market or utility must recognize who is buying. extrapolation

4. Through the stack

IDENTITY→ INTELLIGENCE→ ENERGY→ PROPERTY→ TRANSACTION→ PHYSICAL AGENCY→ COORDINATION

Identity

Humans have IDs and accounts; corporations have legal personality; devices often have certificates and cloud accounts owned by a vendor. current Cryptographic identity (keys; DIDs, or decentralized identifiers; attestations, or signed claims about identity or state) is one research path for persistent machine identity beyond “row in vendor DB.” current extrapolation Failure: spoofing, key loss, identity without accountability.

Intelligence

Unit R meters and decides: buy power now, wait, draw storage, or curtail. extrapolation Industrial AI already supports decisions under uncertainty; closed-loop economic action with physical consequences is the harder step. current Failure: wrong objective — smarter optimization fails faster.

Energy — Power as a Primitive

Without watts, “intelligent” and “on-chain” are decorative. Electricity systems already coordinate via price, tariffs, interconnection, and protection. current Unit R must locate, price, acquire, store, optimize power. See Power as a Primitive. extrapolation on machine-as-buyer; five verbs are ops practice today. Rule: no watts → no agency.

Property → Transaction

Who owns the battery? Who is the legal account holder on the meter? If Unit R “pays,” is the counterparty a utility CIS account under a corporate shell — how almost everything works today current — or a wallet and machine-readable contract without that shell? speculation Ordinary retail bill pay often does not need a blockchain; card rails, ACH, and enterprise APIs already move money. current

Physical agency

Economic intent meets kinematics, safety envelopes, and protective relays. current Physics and protection can veto a profitable plan. That is the world, not a bug in the thought experiment.

Coordination — Electronic Governor

Markets, protocols, APIs, smart contracts where earned, shared-rule systems for mixed human–machine groups, AI agents, and human institutions with veto — law, operators, owners, kill-switches. See The Electronic Governor. This does not argue that government disappears. current + speculation mix; editorial stance on institutions.

5. Protocols that earn their keep

Candidates (where a ledger might earn complexity): persistent identity across organizational boundaries; commitments multiple parties can verify without one operator’s database; coordination among agents that do not share a trusted admin. speculation

Costume: a token ticker on a problem a spreadsheet, an API, and a legal entity already solve — especially ordinary bill pay. current If Unit R’s “electric bill” is really a corporate utility account with automation on top, say so.

Those labels — bill pay, costume, candidates, and what stays open — are lined up in When does a ledger earn its keep? That note does not add a finding.

6. Skepticism — ledger vs rails vs legal

  • Ledger: useful when multi-party trust, portable identity, or hostile environments justify complexity — not by default.
  • Rails: ACH, cards, utility CIS, enterprise APIs already settle retail power for legal persons. current
  • Legal entity: liability, interconnection, and customer-of-record rules still point at humans or corporations in most jurisdictions. current
  • Physical: interconnection, protection trips, and missing stack layers cancel the science-fiction ending. Storage buffers autonomy; it does not abolish the grid.

Also see the full layer table on The stack.

7. What stays visible

When physics vetoes a profitable plan, the useful next question is what the desk still keeps in view — a limit, a stale feed, a schedule that no longer reconciles. This note is an independent exhibition thought experiment. It is not a product you can join.

Where criticism belongs

What fails first: customer-of-record rules, key custody, interconnection, liability, or the energy layer itself? Pushback preferred over applause. Curiosity over prophecy.
