Exhibition note · claim-labeled · curiosity over prophecy

Customer of record

established current hypothesis open

A wallet on a screen is not the account the meter thinks is buying. Who that account names, when a machine “pays,” stays open. This page lines up labels already on the exhibition. It does not pick human, corporation, or machine, and it is not a legal opinion.

Wallet ≠ meter account

hypothesis A machine can show a wallet on a screen. That display is not the same sentence as the meter account, or the utility CIS account, that ordinary bill pay already names. The Power essay opens on the first half: a wallet on a screen; watts still come from a physical system. The ledger note holds the other: a wallet on a screen does not locate power. This page points both at the account name. “Not the same sentence” is the hypothesis. Not a finding that any utility has refused a wallet, and not a comparison of products.

Who ordinary bill pay already names

established current Ordinary retail bill pay often does not need a blockchain. Card rails, ACH, utility CIS, and enterprise APIs already settle retail power for legal persons. Liability, interconnection, and customer-of-record rules still point at humans or corporations in most jurisdictions. That pairing is already exhibition framing: the robot-bill note tags it current; the Power essay tags the same idea established. This page repeats it. It does not survey jurisdictions, and it does not name a statute.

current If a machine’s “electric bill” is really a corporate utility account with automation on top, the honest sentence is to say so. The ledger note already says that about the exhibition’s Unit R. Saying so names the shell that is doing the work. It does not close the question of who the account is.

Costume, if the ticker is glued on

current Costume, as this exhibition uses the word, is a token ticker on a problem a spreadsheet, an API, and a legal entity already solve — especially ordinary bill pay. That is an editorial stance already on the robot-bill note. It is not a census of projects, and it is not a market statistic.

hypothesis A later path might involve wallets and machine-readable contracts without that corporate shell. The robot-bill note tags that alternative speculation. The Power essay and the ledger note tag the same path hypothesis, and mark costume as the failure mode beside it: a token ticker glued onto ordinary bill pay the rails and a legal person already settle. The wallet on the screen is still not the meter account. Hypothesis is not a shipping plan, and it is not a recommendation to build.

What stays open

open Who is the customer of record when a machine “pays” — the machine, the owner, or a legal shell? Power as a Primitive asks the same thing under Acquire: who, or what, is the legal and economic counterparty when “a robot buys power”? The verb is on the page. The answer is not.

open Until that is clear, “paying its own bill” remains agency with a human attached. That sentence is the Power essay’s, not a new legal conclusion.

open What fails first if the guess is wrong — customer-of-record rules, key custody, interconnection, liability, or the energy layer itself? The robot-bill note and the Power essay already list those candidates. This page does not rank them, and it does not say which one arrives.

No timetable sits under the open question. No forecast that a machine becomes the account, or that the account stays a human forever. Curiosity over prophecy includes leaving the name blank.

What would make this wrong

  • Wrong if “rules point at humans or corporations” is read as a statute this page looked up, or as a checklist of jurisdictions. The sentence is carried from the robot-bill note. Nothing here was researched as law.
  • Wrong if the open question is read as “the machine is already the customer,” or as “machines are barred forever.”
  • Wrong if “wallet ≠ meter account” is read as “a wallet never moves money,” or as “a CIS account is a wallet.” The hypothesis is that they are not the same sentence.
  • Wrong if costume is read as a count, a ban, or a claim about a named project. Nothing here was surveyed.
  • Wrong if “agency with a human attached” is read as moral or legal personhood for machines. The Power essay forbids that upgrade.
  • Wrong if a reader takes a product, a token, a signup, or a legal opinion from this page. None are offered.

That humility beat is the one EXPLORE marks as what could make a frame wrong. Beat 02 on that page is what is already true. This note tries to stay inside that pair — curiosity, not prophecy.

Acquire, before the machine

On Power as a Primitive, Acquire asks who, or what, is the counterparty when “a robot buys power.” This note is that question, written beside the ledger note’s open line and the robot-bill skepticism section. The longer claim-labeled draft is the Power essay. The name on the account stays open. Nothing here is a thing you can join.

Where criticism belongs

If the guess about who the account names is the thing that fails first — or if a wallet and a meter account really are the same sentence on some desk — that is the correction. Pushback preferred over applause. There is no comment box, membership, or mailing list on this note. Research intake is not open.
