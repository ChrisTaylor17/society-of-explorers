# Our Christian fellowship covenant

Draft for owner and human editorial review. Prepared with AI assistance. This covenant belongs within Society of Explorers alongside its broader founding manifesto.

## We gather around Jesus Christ

We come to read Scripture, pray, learn and serve in the name of Jesus Christ. We confess him as Lord and Messiah and place our hope in his resurrection. This is our Christian commitment, offered honestly as faith. We want that commitment to become patience, courage, repentance and love in ordinary life.

## We make room at the table

Christians from different churches and traditions are welcome, as are Jewish, Muslim, other religious and nonreligious neighbors who want a respectful conversation. Nobody must pretend to believe, recite a creed or join a prayer to be treated with dignity. A person may listen, pass or leave. Fellowship grows through freely given trust.

## We read before we argue

We read a passage in its literary setting, hear the voices it first addressed and notice what comes before and after. We name the translation and preserve its words. We distinguish a quotation from an allusion, an author’s statement from our inference, and Christian interpretation from a conclusion everyone must accept. A compelling parallel is an invitation to investigate.

## We seek truth with an open Bible and an open hand

We bring our strongest convictions and remain willing to correct our reasoning. Catholic, Orthodox, Protestant and Biblical Unitarian readings should be described in terms their thoughtful adherents would recognize. Root Kernel contributes a named editorial perspective, not a neutral oracle. We ask what supports a claim, what challenges it and what we still do not know.

## We honor Israel’s Scriptures and our neighbors

The Old Testament is also the living Scripture of the Jewish people. We take Jewish interpretation seriously on its own terms. We study the suffering of Jesus without assigning collective guilt to Jews or turning disagreement into contempt. The command to love our neighbor reaches beyond our fellowship, our party and our country.

## We put the person before the platform

People deserve a private space for conscience, questions and growth, and a voluntary commons for what they choose to share. We ask permission before recording, quoting a private conversation or sending someone’s words to an AI service. Prayer and vulnerable disclosures are not raw material for content. Trust is a responsibility we earn.

## We use tools without surrendering judgment

AI can help us find connections, organize sources and formulate objections. It can also invent references, flatten differences and sound certain when it is wrong. Its analysis must show its sources and its limits. We keep Scripture, interpretation and historical reasoning distinct, label drafts, preserve corrections and leave responsibility with people.

## We practice fellowship

A salon is a small commitment to return: learn names, read together, share the floor, ask a careful question and follow through on a useful act of service. Hosts make space for prayer without coercion and disagreement without humiliation. We want curiosity to become friendship, and friendship to become care for real people.

## We remain part of a larger Society

This Christian fellowship belongs within Society of Explorers alongside its wider work in learning, human sovereignty, creativity and community. It adds a place to pursue Christian faith and inquiry. It does not require every Explorer to share one theology or replace the Society’s founding vision. We invite honest company on the way.
